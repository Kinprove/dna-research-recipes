## Sources

Sources checked 2026-10-07. Practitioner cases supply examples and failure modes; their reported family connections and test outcomes are not independently established here. All discussion is original synthesis and paraphrase; third-party prose is cited, not reproduced.

- **S1.** Jarrett Ross, [Researching My Ukrainian Jewish Ancestry Live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NsdlMwZX9yg), 2025-06-14. A marked hypothesis tree directs further record work and possible patrilineal testing while repeated names leave assignments unresolved. Conditional propagation and rollback are the synthesis in this recipe.
- **S2.** Jarrett Ross, [Kiev Genealogy Research Using DNA Results — VLOG 20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n3cqciU14qs), 2017-11-30. A proposed brother connection remains unresolved amid conflicting father's names and DNA observations; the case does not establish a civil-name equivalence.
- **S3.** Jarrett Ross, [Nunes Vaz: A Sephardic Jewish Family](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bo__I7ggTis), 2019-10-19. Separately traced branches and proposed connections guide documentary and Y-DNA work. The presenter's certainty is not adopted as proof of a named ancestor or route.
- **S4.** Lara Diamond, [Chromosome mapping and endogamy](https://blog.dnapainter.com/blog/chromosome-mapping-and-endogamy/), 2022-01-30. The worked cousin example distinguishes a known documentary relationship from segment contributions through other ancestry. No universal segment cutoff is derived here.
- **S5.** Jarrett Ross, [Identifying a Town of Origin for Jewish Genealogy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g5aMe7oUW_A), 2021-08-04. Candidate household information guides follow-up searches; several displayed death indexes derive from the same records.
- **S6.** Janette Silverman, [Jewish Names — RootsTech 2026 handout](https://cms-z-assets.familysearch.org/f2/5a/1ffc05f42cc978e2a2ef0d5b0c22/jewish-names-handout-2026.pdf), page 1. Naming customs vary by community and family; deceased-relative naming is a general Ashkenazi custom.
- **S7.** Adina Newman, [Jewish Genealogy 101 — RootsTech 2025 handout](https://cms-z-assets.familysearch.org/7d/67/4a9454556c83aa8e208aae0af180/jewishgenhandoutrootstech2025.pdf), page 1. Sephardi children may be named after living relatives; this is not a claim about every family.
- **S8.** JRI-Poland, [Books of Residents and Census Records](https://jri-poland.org/blog/books-residents-census-records/), 2020-04-15. Its pre-1932 legal-residence distinction is collection-specific.
- **S9.** Katy Rowe-Schurwanz, FamilyTreeDNA, [How to Interpret DNA Matches — Family Finder Guide](https://blog.familytreedna.com/interpreting-family-finder-matches/), 2026-04-09. Endogamy and multiple common ancestors affect relationship predictions. Checked 2026-10-07.
- **S10.** JewishGen, [Frequently asked questions](https://www.jewishgen.org/InfoFiles/faq.html), section on translating Americanized given names. No fixed reverse translation. Checked 2026-10-07.
- **S11.** Serafima Velkovich, [Tracing Jewish Family Roots: Genealogy, Memory, and Identity](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YUxBa_g6QGI), presentation 2026-06-20, published 2026-06-22. Community-specific naming examples motivated the conditional naming search; the qualified factual rule here is checked against S6 and S7, rather than adopting the presentation's absolutes.
