# AncestryDNA: eight practical ways to investigate matches

## Goal

Find a target's family even when its own tree is missing, then pursue the connection to your family. The key shortcut is to work the target's relatives in order of **cM to the target**, rather than cM to you.

Read [ai-for-dna-research.md](ai-for-dna-research.md) for cM ranges, endogamy and segment interpretation, and [unknown-parentage-wato.md](unknown-parentage-wato.md) for placement, competing hypotheses and choosing a tester. Use [ai-for-documentary-records.md](ai-for-documentary-records.md) for record retrieval and [evidence-proof-judgment.md](evidence-proof-judgment.md) for the final proof argument.

## Access and inputs

The ordinary shared-match preview shows up to three people without membership; family-history membership unlocks the full list. Enhanced Shared Matches adds match-to-match cM with Pro Tools. That enhanced website view is available in the US, Canada, Australia and UK. Obtain a missing comparison from an authorized kit manager when your own access cannot show it.

## 1. Find the target through its closest relatives

**Use when:** the target has no useful tree.

Open Enhanced Shared Matches and sort by cM **to the target**. Start with its highest-sharing relatives, including people far down your own match list. Enhanced Shared Matches lists relatives below the old 20 cM shared-match floor: Bartlett (2024-07-07) found a relative sharing just **9 cM with him but 1773 cM with his 25 cM target**.

Open those relatives' trees and use obituaries, births and census households to identify the target and its parents. At 1773 cM, the [chart-supported candidates](https://dnapainter.com/tools/sharedcmv4) are full/half sibling, aunt/uncle–niece/nephew and grandparent–grandchild paths. If their trees are also missing, use recipe 3.

**Output:** the target's identity and sourced parents or grandparents, obtained through a relative whose tree is informative.

## 2. Work a branch with a small anchor table

**Use when:** several matches appear connected to one ancestral branch.

Start with 2–4 anchors whose most recent common ancestor and descent path are documented. Record cM to you, the dated Ancestry label, cM to each anchor, and the proposed ancestral path.

Use Bartlett's (2024-07-19) pass order: very close target relatives first, then relatives above **90 cM to the target**, then those above **about 50 cM**. Repeat from newly identified anchors. Add an anchor when it separates two open hypotheses.

Orient SideView's initially anonymous Parent 1/Parent 2 split with known relatives. When a tested biological parent matches you, Ancestry labels the split maternal/paternal automatically.

**Output:** a compact comparison table and the next match that distinguishes the competing paths.

## 3. Search collateral names, not just the ancestral surname

**Use when:** the main surname search finds nothing.

Search Ancestry matches for surnames along the descent paths, including spouses who married in, and for birthplaces along those paths (Bartlett, 2024-07-19). A match's married surname can hide the connection that its parent's or grandparent's surname reveals.

Surname and birthplace searches require no membership; viewing tree details does.

**Output:** specific matches and people to trace back to the candidate couple.

## 4. Put the branch code first; make the dots readable

**Use when:** you repeatedly inspect the same matches.

Begin each note with the ancestral branch, followed by review date, basis and next question (Bartlett, 2024-07-19; 2025-01-01). Notes hold 500 characters. The owner and invitees can see them; collaborators and managers can edit them.

```text
M16 hypothesis | reviewed 2026-10-03 | basis: Ruth's tree and census
| next: obtain J.K.'s parent birth record
```

Leeds (2023-03-27) documents group dots following palette order rather than the order of assignment. Leeds's eight-group scheme uses the middle row from right to left, **green through yellow**: paternal cool colors appear ahead of maternal warm colors. Write a legend linking each color to its ancestral branch.

Put a match in each relevant group. Revisit its note when a tree, record, relative or available view changes.

**Output:** a branch you can recognize while scanning a shared list, plus an explicit next task.

## 5. Fix the tester link; search hypotheses with a separate tree

**Use when:** generation counts disagree, or ThruLines supplies no path.

Confirm who actually tested and which tree person the DNA is linked to. A link to the tester's parent or grandparent shifts every generation count (Bartlett, 2026-03-27). The manager can be a different person. Cooper (2023-04-23) explains how linking the correct tree person lets Ancestry propose paths through other members' trees.

Editing a tree leaves DNA matches and cM unchanged; it changes tree-derived paths.

For ThruLines, matching must be enabled, with family-history membership, two-step verification or Apple/Google sign-in, and a linked public or private searchable tree containing identifying details across at least four generations. ThruLines reaches fifth-great-grandparents. Its parent filter uses biological-parent labels in the tree, rather than DNA By Parent.

For an unresolved ancestral path:

1. Preserve the evidenced tree and record the original DNA-to-tree link.
2. Put one candidate lineage in a separate, labeled research tree and link your test to **yourself as the actual tester** there.
3. Allow up to 48 hours after each change. Save newly surfaced candidates, their cM, proposed descents and the hypothesis that generated them.
4. Repeat for another hypothesis, then restore the original link.

Bartlett (2023-07-08) reported **31 new ThruLines candidates** after changing a speculative lineage. Independently document candidates' descents, check the recorded cM against the relationship-range reference, and compare those sourced placements under the competing hypotheses. Mark paths supplied only by the research tree “tree-generated.”

**Output:** a verified tester/link statement and a candidate comparison for each hypothesis.

## 6. Detect a label that already uses your shared-match evidence

**Use when:** an Ancestry relationship label is being used to support a placement.

**Never count a shared-match-informed prediction as independent support for a placement derived from the same graph.** Larkin's 2026-07-24 account identifies the affected range as **~650–2200 cM**, including half-siblings, aunt/uncle/niece/nephew, grandparent/grandchild, first cousins and great-aunt/uncle or great-grandparent relationships.

Keep three columns: observed cM, dated vendor label, and sourced pedigree path. Include half and full alternatives when considering a relationship (Bartlett, 2026-03-27). A changed label belongs in the label/date column; inspect changed measurements, records or tester identity before revising the documented pedigree.

**Output:** a comparison table with affected vendor labels marked “shared-graph-informed.”

## 7. Recover a missing row through another view

**Use when:** a relative is absent from a shared list or cluster.

Standard Matches by Cluster uses **65–1300 cM** to the tester as its window and excludes closest relatives, inter-match links below 20 cM and SideView-unassigned matches.

For a 25 cM target below the standard window, set a **custom cluster's search range to 20–60 cM**, select the target as primary, and add up to four sidekicks shared with it. Save the selections and returned rows. Sidekicks already within the chosen range need not be added separately.

Inspect a missing pair from another authorized kit's perspective (Estes, 2022-05-12).

**Output:** a recovered observation with its kit, view, filters and date, or a request naming the missing tester pair. An absent row is “not shown,” never a measured zero.

## 8. Match the person across sites before using segments

**Use when:** another site's segment data is offered for an Ancestry match.

Compare the tested person's name or initials, manager, tree people and overlapping matches (Bartlett, 2022-07-06). Confirm identity through documented test-registration/upload provenance or the tester/kit manager. Compare Ancestry's **unweighted cM**, the amount before Timber, with the other provider's total, rather than using Ancestry's weighted headline (Larkin, 2021-06-05; updated 2022-03-29).

Keep the attachment record with the provider, private kit reference, date and who confirmed identity. Attach segment data only after that confirmation.

Ancestry has no chromosome browser: its Painter shows ancestry regions rather than match segments. Its download is a genotype text ZIP, not a match-list export, and it accepts no outside DNA uploads.

**Output:** segment evidence tied to a verified tested person on the other platform.

## Worked example

**Invented people and records; the 25, 9 and 1773 cM observations are reported by Bartlett (2024-07-07).**

Ada's 25 cM target J.K. has no tree. Ruth shares 9 cM with Ada but 1773 cM with J.K. Use Ruth's pedigree to construct J.K.'s family.

In this fictional case, Ruth and J.K.'s mother are full siblings. Ruth's tree, birth and household records provide the parents' names, allowing Ada to construct both sides of J.K.'s missing pedigree. Searching paternal collateral surnames finds another descendant with a usable tree. Records establish that Ada and J.K. are both great-great-grandchildren of the same ancestral couple, descending through different children; J.K.'s path runs through his father.

**Result:** Ruth is J.K.'s full maternal aunt, consistent with 1773 cM. Ada and J.K. have a documented third-cousin path through J.K.'s father, consistent with 25 cM in the [Shared cM Project chart](https://dnapainter.com/tools/sharedcmv4). The pedigrees identify their common ancestral couple. Use the close relative to identify the target, then trace both parental families for your own connection.

## Never delegate

- Inventing cM probabilities or relationship ranges: use the linked DNA-range/WATO recipes.
- Calling an Ancestry shared-match group or ThruLine segment triangulation.
- Attaching another site's segments to an unconfirmed profile identity.

The human views membership-gated trees, requests comparisons and confirms who tested.

## Reusable prompt

```text
Investigate this AncestryDNA target using my captured rows, notes and records.
Sort relatives by cM to the TARGET. Find its informative relatives and build
its family from records. Make a small anchor table, search collateral names,
and give the next identifying query. Check the tested person and linked
tree person. Mark labels that reuse shared-match evidence. For a missing
row, name the exact pair and another authorized observation route.
Keep observed cM, dated labels and sourced pedigree paths separate.
Do not invent probabilities or claim Ancestry segment triangulation.
```
